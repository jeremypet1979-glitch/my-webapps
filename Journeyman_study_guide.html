📐 Essential Formulas for the Exam

  • Ohm's Law & Power: $I = \frac{E}{R}$, $P = E \times I$, $P = I^2 \times R$, $R = \frac{E^2}{P}$
  • Single-Phase Voltage Drop ($V_d$): $V_d = \frac{2 \times K \times I \times L}{\text{Cmil}}$ (where $K = 12.9$ for copper, $21.2$ for aluminum).
  • Three-Phase Voltage Drop ($V_d$): $V_d = \frac{1.732 \times K \times I \times L}{\text{Cmil}}$
  • Transformer Ratios: $\frac{E_p}{E_s} = \frac{I_s}{I_p} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}$
  • Power Factor Correction: $\text{kVar} = \sqrt{\text{VA}_{\text{app}}^2 - \text{Watts}^2}$

⚖️ Utah State Electrical Licensing Laws & Administrative Rules

Overview of DOPM (Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing) regulations for Utah electricians.

1. Licensing Classifications & Experience Requirements

  • Journeyman Electrician: Requires completion of an approved electrical apprenticeship program (minimum 4 years / 8,000 hours of approved work experience under a licensed Master Electrician) plus passing score on the Utah Journeyman Electrician exam.
  • Master Electrician: Requires at least 2 years of licensed experience as a Journeyman Electrician (or equivalent supervisory engineering experience) and passing the Master Electrician exam.

2. Continuing Education (CEU) Requirements

  • Utah electricians must complete **16 hours of continuing education** every 2-year licensing cycle (renewing by November 30 of odd-numbered years).
  • At least 12 hours must be Core NEC code updates.

3. Supervision Ratios

  • On any job site, a licensed Master or Journeyman Electrician may supervise apprentice electricians in accordance with Utah state ratio limits to ensure safety and code compliance.
